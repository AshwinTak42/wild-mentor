The landscape

The wild side of Rajasthan

Bijoliya is a town in Rajasthan between two tiger reserves, Ramgarh Vishdhari (RVTR) and Mukundra Hills (MHTR). It is also close to the Bassi Wildlife Sanctuary and the Menal Forest Reserve, which makes it an important area for wildlife movement just outside the protected zones.

The landscape links the Aravalli Hills to the Vindhyachal ranges. It has grasslands, thick forest valleys, open fields and waterfalls, a good home for many kinds of wildlife. After years of exploring this region, Ashwin invites you to see it beyond park boundaries.

Birds

Vultures, raptors and owls

Raptors are the main attraction.

Vultures

Seven species are recorded: four in summer (long-billed, red-headed, white-rumped and Egyptian) and three winter migrants (cinereous, Himalayan and Eurasian griffon).

Owls

Three large owls are seen here: the mottled wood owl, the Indian rock eagle owl and the brown fish owl.

Mammals

Wolves, cats and jackals

Cats

Leopard, jungle cat, rusty-spotted cat and Asiatic wild cat, along with two civets, the Asian palm civet and the small Indian civet.

Wolves

One of the rarest mammals in Rajasthan, the wolf rules the grasslands of Bijoliya.

Jackals and others

Golden jackals are common. Bengal fox, hyena and sloth bear are also part of this landscape and can be seen on safari.

After dark

Herping

Search for snakes and lizards at night, on foot and with torches, with Ashwin to identify what you find.

More to explore

Rock paintings, waterfalls and camping

Rock paintings

Rock paintings hidden in the forest include the Bird Raider painting, one of the biggest rock paintings in Rajasthan.

Waterfalls

Several seasonal waterfalls are in the area, usually flowing from July to February. One of the widest in Rajasthan sits in a deep valley rich in plants and wildlife, and carries the history of ancient humans.

Camping and bonfire

Camp in the middle of the forest for a real and raw wildlife experience.
