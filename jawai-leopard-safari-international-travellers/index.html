Jawai Leopard Safari for International Travellers

Jawai is one of the few places in India where leopards live in open granite hills and can be watched in daylight. This guide answers the questions international travellers ask most before they book.

Where is Jawai?

Jawai is in Pali district in southern Rajasthan, between Udaipur and Jodhpur. The area is built around the Jawai Dam and the rugged granite hills of the Aravalli range.

It is not a fenced national park. Leopards live in the hills and caves close to villages, farmland and the reservoir, which is why sightings here feel so different from a typical Indian safari.

How to reach Jawai

Most international travellers fly into Delhi or Mumbai and connect to Udaipur (roughly 145–150 km from Jawai, about 3 to 3.5 hours by road) or Jodhpur (roughly 170 km, about 4 hours).

The nearest railway station is Jawai Bandh, about 10 km from the safari area, with connections to cities such as Jaipur and Jodhpur. Public transport beyond the station is limited, so most visitors arrange a private car. Ashwin can help you plan the route.

Best time to visit Jawai

October to March is the most comfortable and most popular season. Days are dry and cool, the vegetation is sparse, and in winter migratory birds gather at the dam.

Leopards live here all year. April to June is hot, but dawn and dusk drives can still be rewarding. July to September brings the monsoon: the hills turn green, tracks are harder to use and sightings are less predictable.

How many days do you need?

Two days is enough for a first taste of Jawai. If photography is your priority, add nights. More drives mean more chances to wait for good light and behaviour.

Leopards are wild animals. A sighting is likely, never guaranteed, and anyone who promises otherwise is selling something.

Safari timings

Safaris run twice a day: early morning from around sunrise, and late afternoon into the evening. Start times move with the season, because sunrise is much earlier in summer than in winter, and they are confirmed when you book.

These are also the hours when leopards are most active, so the early start is worth it.

What wildlife can you see?

  • Leopards resting on boulders, in caves and along ridgelines.
  • Marsh crocodiles basking on the banks of the Jawai Dam.
  • Birds: peafowl year-round, and in winter migratory birds on the dam, including flamingos and pelicans.
  • Shyer animals: striped hyena, sloth bear, jackal and jungle cat live in the region but are seen far less often.

What camera and lens should you bring?

A telephoto zoom does most of the work: 100–400 mm or 150–600 mm on a full-frame camera, or 70–300 mm on a crop-sensor body. Leopards are often a fair distance away on the rocks.

Add a standard or wide zoom (24–70 mm) for the granite hills, the dam and village scenes. Bring spare batteries and memory cards, a lens cloth for dust, and binoculars. A beanbag or clamp works better than a tripod in a jeep. Shoot at a fast shutter speed for any moving animal.

No big lens? Binoculars and a phone still make for a memorable safari.

Jawai vs Ranthambore

JawaiRanthambore
Main animalLeopardTiger
SettingOpen granite hills, caves, a dam, village landNational park with forest, lakes and an old fort
FeelNo gates or fences, fewer vehiclesFixed zones and routes, busier
RegionSouthern Rajasthan (Pali district)Eastern Rajasthan (Sawai Madhopur)

They are in different parts of Rajasthan, so most travellers pair them in a longer itinerary instead of a short trip. Choose Jawai for leopards and landscape, and Ranthambore for tigers.

Where to stay

Options near Bera and the Jawai Dam run from simple lodges to luxury camps. Choose somewhere close to the hills so your drives start early. Ashwin can help arrange your stay and suggest places that suit your budget.

What to expect

You travel in an open jeep with a local guide who reads pugmarks, alarm calls and the behaviour of other animals. Sightings often come after patient waiting, and the landscape is rough and dusty.

Mornings are cold in winter and very warm in summer, so dress in layers and muted earth tones, and carry a hat, sunscreen and water. Keep the packing light.

Responsible wildlife viewing

  • Keep your distance and let the animal decide how close it is.
  • Never bait or call animals, and do not use flash.
  • Speak quietly and stay in the vehicle unless your guide says otherwise.
  • Leave no litter, and do not fly drones.
  • Respect the villagers who share this land, and choose operators that work with local communities.

Sample 2-day itinerary

Day 1

Arrive in Jawai, check in and have lunch. Briefing with Ashwin on the landscape, leopard behaviour and how the safari works. Optional sunset at the Jawai Dam, then dinner.

Day 2

Early start for the safari in the granite hills. Breakfast, then an optional dam or village visit before you depart.

Dates, group size and photography needs change the plan.
